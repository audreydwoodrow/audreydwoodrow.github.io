I am an undergraduate studying Computer Science at Stanford, with a minor in Education, graduating in 2029. My work sits where those two things meet: building software that is genuinely useful, and helping other people learn to build it.

As a section leader for CS198, I teach a weekly section of twelve students in Stanford's introductory CS sequence and grade their assignments and exams. Most of the job is coaching people through debugging and program design — recursion, algorithmic analysis, object-oriented programming, core data structures — and most of the skill is knowing when to answer a question and when to hand it back.

Outside of coursework I build things for problems I actually have: a neural network that predicts how good a ski day will be, a dashboard that pulls my coursework into one calendar, an outfit matcher that runs entirely on my own machine. They are small on purpose. I care about software that people can read, run, and keep.
